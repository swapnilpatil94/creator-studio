## What the research found (and where sources disagree)
- Yudhishthira, Duryodhana and the hell test: Svargarohana Parva ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svargarohana_Parva)); the hell was an illusion made by the gods and the dog was Dharma. Sources differ on small details.
- The iron pestle and the end of Krishna: Mausala Parva ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausala_Parva), [Ganguli translation](https://mahabharataonline.com/translation/mahabharata_16004.php)). Who cursed the Yadavas differs (sages in the Mausala Parva, Gandhari in the Stri Parva). That the hunter Jara was Vali reborn is a later tradition.
- Rama, Lakshmana and Kala: Uttara Kanda ([Durvasa, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durvasa)). How Lakshmana's life ends differs between retellings.
- Ravana's curse by Nalakubara: Uttara Kanda 26 ([Nalakuvara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalakuvara), [Rambha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambha_(apsara))). The popular line "he could never touch an unwilling woman" is a gloss; the text says his head would split if he forced a woman.
- Ekalavya was killed by Krishna: Drona Parva names it, the Harivamsa adds details ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekalavya)). The "stabbed in the back" version has no text support.
- Harishchandra: Markandeya Purana ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harishchandra)); the buyer is a chandala in most versions, a brahmin in some.
- Dasharatha's curse: Shravan Kumar ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravana_Kumara)); check the exact kanda in a critical edition.
- Ashwatthama: Sauptika Parva ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauptika_Parva)); the 3,000 years and the forehead jewel details vary.
- Iravan and the Koovagam festival are a regional tradition ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/koovagam-koothandavar-temple-festival)); label it, and treat the transgender community's tradition with respect.
- Barbarika: the Skanda Purana attribution is repeated in popular sources but I could not confirm the khanda ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatu_Shyam_Temple)).

## Known gaps in this plan
- Festival stories (Dhanteras, Diwali, Gita Jayanti, Makar Sankranti and so on) are not matched because this plan is untold-only; the planner's older festival logic still holds if you want one story swapped in on those days.
- Dark humour is thin on purpose: the best candidates are Vali carrying Ravana under his arm and the 25 Baital riddles; most of the rest are solemn.
